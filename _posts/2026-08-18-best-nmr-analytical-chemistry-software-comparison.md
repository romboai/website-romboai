  ## Choosing analytical chemistry software in 2026

  Labs now choose among three types of analytical chemistry software. Instrument-native tools connect acquisition and processing within one vendor's hardware. Vendor-neutral suites process data across instrument brands and analytical techniques. AI-native platforms automate spectral interpretation across varied instruments and sample types.

  Four criteria separate these options. Vendor-agnostic support measures how broadly each platform accepts and interprets data across instrument makers. Routine use fit reflects how well the software handles recurring acquisition, processing, reporting, and review.

  Deep research fit covers advanced experiments, custom methods, and detailed manual control. Automation measures how much spectroscopy software can reduce repetitive analysis and apply consistent interpretation across larger data volumes. A single-vendor lab may favor acquisition integration, while a mixed fleet may place more weight on format support and automated interpretation.

  One question comes before all four: what decision the software output feeds. A processed spectrum, a confirmed structure and a property value that goes into a release or blending decision are different deliverables, and no single tool in this guide produces all three.

  ## Rombo AI: the AI-native, foundation-model option

  Rombo AI represents the AI-native tier of analytical chemistry software, with two distinct products. The [Rombo AI Platform](/product/) predicts physico-chemical properties from quantitative NMR, benchmarks each property against the laboratory's reference method and tracks drift over time. [Spectra](/spectra/) addresses a different question, compound identification and structure elucidation, by ranking candidate structures from NMR data. The two share a modelling approach, not a feature set.

  Both build on a foundation model that Rombo AI describes as pre-trained on millions of spectra. Traditional chemometric models usually require calibration data that match a specific instrument and sample class, so a new spectrometer, facility or material can require another calibration cycle; our guide to [calibration transfer](/blog/calibration-transfer-spectroscopy-models-break-instruments-change) explains why. A pre-trained model gives each deployment a shared starting point and needs less use-case calibration data. It does not remove validation: each property still has to be checked against the reference method on independent samples, and checked again on each new instrument.

  That approach suits labs with mixed instrument fleets and multiple sites. Rombo AI describes one interpretation layer across data from Bruker, JEOL, Agilent/Varian and Oxford Instruments systems, covered in our article on [multi-instrument NMR compatibility](/blog/NMR_instrument_compatibility) and in the research behind the [foundation model](/blog/rose_foundation_model_NMR_spectral_data). Declared compatibility is a starting point: for a given spectrometer, confirm the data format and validate the model on that instrument before relying on it.

  Rombo AI states that its workflow goes from sample to multi-property report in [about 15 minutes](https://rombo.ai). That is a company figure for its own workflow, not an independent benchmark. What changes most is the model-building and calibration effort for each new analytical problem. The chemist's review and the laboratory's reference methods stay in place.

  What Rombo AI does not do: it does not control acquisition (TopSpin, JASON or the benchtop vendor's software still run the spectrometer), it is not a general processing suite like Mnova or Spectrus, and it does not replace the reference methods its property models are validated against.

  ## Mnova (Mestrelab): the multi-technique research standard

  Mnova gives research labs one multi-vendor environment for several analytical techniques. Its modules cover NMR, LC-MS, GC-MS, and electronic and vibrational spectroscopy through ElViS. Researchers can process spectra, combine results across techniques, annotate data, and prepare reports for publication in the same desktop platform. [University of York describes Mnova](https://www.york.ac.uk/it-services/tools/mestrenova/) as a multivendor software suite rather than software tied to one instrument maker.

  Academic institutions often provide Mnova through site or network licenses. For example, the University of Rochester funds access for faculty, staff, and students, while users connect the installed client to a university license server. The university also specifies a particular software version for compatibility, which shows how local IT policies can shape deployment. [Rochester positions Mnova](https://tech.rochester.edu/software/mestrenova-mnova/) as shared research infrastructure across chemistry disciplines.

  Mnova is also more automated than its desktop reputation suggests. Mestrelab's [Gears plugin](https://mestrelab.com/main-product/gears-verify) builds automation workflows for analytical data, and Gears Verify runs structure verification on the Mnova Verify engine for data acquired in real time or in large batches, with automatic reports. Mnova fits labs that value broad processing, reporting and rule-based automation across mixed analytical data, with the chemist defining each workflow.

  ## TopSpin (Bruker): the acquisition-integrated choice for Bruker fleets

  TopSpin best serves labs that acquire most of their NMR data on Bruker instruments. Bruker connects spectrometer control, experiment setup, acquisition, and processing within one environment, which reduces handoffs between instrument software and separate analysis tools.

  Bruker states that the [processing version of TopSpin is free for academic users](https://www.bruker.com/en/products-and-solutions/mr/nmr-software/topspin.html). Offline, TopSpin can also process [data from Agilent, Varian and JEOL systems and JCAMP files](https://nmr.nd.edu/software/), but cross-format processing does not provide the same acquisition integration on third-party instruments.

  Bruker presents SpinPilot in TopSpin 5 as new automation software that makes routine acquisition steps more consistent and extends automation across a lab. The available support for [SpinPilot's automation capabilities](https://www.bruker.com/en/products-and-solutions/mr/nmr-software/topspin.html) comes from Bruker's own product material, so buyers should confirm the supported experiments and deployment requirements directly. Commercial pricing remains non-public.

  ## ACD/Labs NMR Suite (Spectrus Processor): the format-agnostic integrator

  Spectrus Processor fits labs that need one informatics layer for analytical data produced by mixed instrument fleets. The [Spectrus Platform supports major instrument vendor formats](https://www.selectscience.net/company/advanced-chemistry-development-inc-acd-labs), along with open formats such as JCAMP, ASCII, and Allotrope ADF. A lab can therefore process data without maintaining a separate workflow for each instrument brand.

  ACD/Labs also combines NMR with other analytical techniques in a common interface. That multi-technique approach helps industrial labs assemble related measurements when identifying compounds or managing analytical records.

  Spectrus extends beyond desktop processing through automated solutions and custom enterprise projects. These capabilities suit organizations that need analytical knowledge management across departments or sites. By comparison, instrument-native software usually provides a tighter connection to acquisition hardware, while Spectrus concentrates on cross-vendor ingestion and enterprise automation.

  ## JASON (JEOL): the JEOL-instrument specialist with automation extensions

  JASON fits JEOL-native labs that want processing and structural analysis close to their instrument environment. JEOL develops JASON as its NMR processing and analysis software, and the [JASON 3.1 release](https://www.jeoljason.com/2023/11/13/jason-3-1-expanding-capabilities-and-automation/) shows where its depth lies.

  JASON offers particular depth for DOSY and quantitative NMR. Its DOSY tools fit diffusion data and calculate corrected diffusion time from pulse-sequence parameters. The SMILEQ plugin supports multiple references, internal and external standards, and ISO 24583-compliant uncertainty calculations.

  BeautifulJASON provides the main automation extension. The [Python package](https://www.jeoljason.com/2023/11/13/jason-3-1-expanding-capabilities-and-automation/) lets you call JASON functions inside custom lab workflows and analytical data projects. JEOL also separates Academia and Industry plans, although public pricing remains unavailable. JASON makes the most sense when JEOL integration, DOSY, and qNMR outweigh the need for documented cross-vendor support.

  ## Matching the tool to your lab profile

  If your academic lab uses one instrument brand, choose the vendor-native row. TopSpin fits Bruker fleets, while JASON fits JEOL fleets and adds Python automation for custom routines.

  If you manage a multi-instrument industrial fleet, prioritize software that handles spectra across vendors and sites. ACD/Labs suits enterprises focused on multi-format ingestion and informatics, Mnova suits labs that want to script their own processing workflows, and Rombo AI fits labs that want interpretation from a pre-trained model rather than a model they build and maintain per instrument.   ## For energy and QC labs: when the output is a property, not a spectrum

  A refinery, terminal or utility laboratory usually needs numbers it can act on: API gravity and sulfur at crude receipt, or water content in transformer oil before a maintenance decision. Processing suites give you a clean spectrum and good tools to read it. They do not, on their own, give you a property value with a known error against your ASTM or IEC method. That takes a calibration or a validated model.

  That is the gap the Rombo AI Platform addresses: several properties from one benchtop NMR scan, each compared with the reference method on paired samples. See the [crude oil monitoring use case](/use-cases/crude-oil-monitoring/) for refinery properties and the [transformer oil testing guide](/blog/transformer-oil-testing-properties-methods-nmr) for insulating oil. Whichever tool you consider, ask for the same evidence before a pilot:

  | Question to ask | Why it matters |
  | --- | --- |
  | Which properties, against which reference method? | A model is validated per property, not per product. |
  | How many independent samples, and from which range? | The validation holds only inside the range the samples cover. |
  | Which metric and acceptance limit, agreed before results? | Limits set after the data are seen are not a test. |
  | What happens on a second instrument or after a service? | Line shape and referencing change; the model must be checked again. |
  | Who reviews the result, and what is the fallback? | The reference method stays the reference for disputes and out-of-range samples. |

  ## FAQs

  - **Is NMR software pricing public?** Mostly not. Rombo AI and the four other vendors in this guide quote prices on request. The exception is academic use: Bruker states that the [TopSpin processing version is free for academic users](https://www.bruker.com/en/products-and-solutions/mr/nmr-software/topspin.html). Quotes usually depend on seats, modules and support.
  - **Can one tool replace instrument-native software?** Not for acquisition. Instrument-native software controls the spectrometer. Rombo AI works on the acquired spectra and leaves acquisition to TopSpin, JASON or the benchtop vendor's software. Most labs keep both.
  - **What does vendor-agnostic mean in practice?** The software reads and interprets data from several instrument brands. In practice, confirm the data formats for your instruments and validate results on each one, because differences in line shape and referencing affect any model; see our guide to [calibration transfer](/blog/calibration-transfer-spectroscopy-models-break-instruments-change).
  - **How does AI-native modeling differ from chemometric processing?** AI-native models learn reusable patterns from large spectral collections before they see your samples. Rombo AI's foundation model is pre-trained on millions of spectra (a company figure), while a chemometric model such as PLS is built for one use case from your own calibration set. Pre-training reduces the calibration data needed; it does not remove validation.
  - **Does Rombo AI replace Mnova or TopSpin?** No. Rombo AI adds interpretation or property prediction on top of an existing workflow, while Mnova or TopSpin keeps handling processing or acquisition. You can add it without rebuilding every workflow.
  - **Which Rombo AI product answers which question?** The [Rombo AI Platform](/product/) answers "what are this sample's properties?" for energy and industrial labs. [Spectra](/spectra/) answers "which compound or structure is this?" for analytical chemists. They are separate products with separate evaluations.
  - **How should a lab evaluate an AI property model before buying?** Agree the properties, reference methods, sample set and acceptance limits before any result, then test on samples the model has not seen. The checklist in the section above is a starting point, and it is how our [feasibility review](/nmr-feasibility/) works.
