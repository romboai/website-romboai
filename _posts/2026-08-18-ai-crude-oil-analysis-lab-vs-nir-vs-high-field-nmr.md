  ## Why crude oil analysis method choice matters now

  Crude oil analysis determines how a refinery values, blends, and processes each crude type. Refinery teams compare methods on cost, turnaround time, measurement performance, and operational complexity. A method suited to certification may delay routine decisions, while a fast screening method may provide less compositional detail. Where refineries sample crude and which decisions each analysis supports is covered in [Why Crude Oil Analysis Matters at Every Stage of the Refinery](https://rombo.ai/blog/crude-oil-analysis-refinery-process-economics).

  - **Laboratory testing** measures crude properties through established physical and chemical procedures, making it suitable for formal assays and certification.
  - **NIR** estimates properties by relating near-infrared spectra to calibrated reference samples, which supports rapid screening of familiar crude types.
  - **High-Field NMR** produces detailed molecular spectra for deep structural analysis, but specialized instruments and expertise increase operating complexity.
  - **Rombo AI** combines benchtop NMR with pre-trained machine learning models to convert spectra into property estimates for routine monitoring. The models are designed to transfer across instruments and sites; transfer still has to be checked on your own instruments.

  The right choice depends on whether you need an authoritative assay, quick screening of a known stream, detailed molecular analysis, or repeatable monitoring of several properties.

  ## How the comparison is evaluated

  Each method is evaluated across cost, turnaround time, measurement performance, and operational complexity. Cost includes instrument purchases, external testing fees, calibration, software, and specialist labor. Turnaround runs from sample receipt to a decision-ready result. Measurement performance considers repeatability and agreement with reference values across crude types.

  Operational complexity reflects staffing, calibration, sample preparation, and maintenance. Low complexity requires routine operators and limited recalibration. Medium complexity requires trained staff or periodic model updates. High complexity requires specialist interpretation, extensive preparation, or frequent instrument-specific calibration. The comparison reflects refinery operating use rather than regulatory acceptance or research capability.

  ## Cost: lab fees, instrument capex, and per-sample economics

  Laboratory testing creates recurring expense because each new crude needs its own assay, and repeat checks add more. A comprehensive assay combines whole-crude tests with distillation into cuts and separate analyses of each cut, so its cost grows with the number of cuts and properties requested. Prices vary with the panel and the laboratory, so base comparisons on a quote for the panel you actually need rather than on a generic figure.

  NIR replaces much of the external testing expense with instrument ownership and calibration work. Each model needs laboratory reference data that connects measured spectra to properties such as density or sulfur content. Once a model covers a stable crude family, the cost per additional sample can become low. New crude types or changing compositions may require more reference assays and calibration work.

  High-field NMR carries the largest equipment and facility burden of the instrument-based options. Refineries must account for the spectrometer, installation requirements, service contracts, specialist staff, and supporting laboratory infrastructure. High sample volume can spread those fixed costs across more analyses, but low utilization leaves a high effective cost per sample.

  ## Turnaround time: weeks, hours, or minutes

  A fully executed crude assay may take several weeks from start to finish, and the distillation work alone can take multiple days, according to the testing company [AmSpec](https://www.amspecgroup.com/resources/inside-the-crude-oil-assay-laboratory-science-behind-the-samples/). Laboratory queues and report review extend the sample-to-answer clock further. Individual tests, such as density, can be fast; it is the full characterization that tends to arrive after the operating decision has been made. A refinery may then rely on an earlier assay while the new results remain pending.

  NIR produces a spectrum within seconds or minutes, but calibration determines when you receive a usable answer. A crude type outside the calibration set may require reference testing and model updates. Those extra steps can turn a rapid scan into an hours-long or multi-day workflow.

  High-field NMR can acquire detailed spectra within minutes or hours, depending on the experiment. Sample preparation, instrument availability, and specialist interpretation add more time. Recalibration or method changes can create further delays when the crude composition or instrument configuration changes.

  Rombo describes a sample-to-report time of about **15 minutes** for supported workflows on benchtop NMR. That figure is a company-reported capability rather than a guarantee for every matrix: sample transport, preparation, and review add latency, and a 15-minute report is not the same as continuous online monitoring. Where the shorter cycle fits inside the decision window, a refinery can adjust blending or processing decisions during the same operating period rather than after the crude has already moved through the facility.

  ## Precision and accuracy across crude types

  A single accuracy percentage cannot describe performance across density, sulfur content, viscosity, and distillation yields, or across every crude grade. A useful performance statement names the property, the range of samples tested, the error metric (for example, root-mean-square error of prediction on an independent validation set), and the primary test method used as the reference.

  Traditional laboratory assays remain the reference for certified composition data because each test measures a defined property through an established procedure. Their accuracy depends on sampling, preparation, and the chosen method, but they provide the benchmark used to validate faster techniques.

  The reference method is also the yardstick. A predicted value cannot be more reliable than the reference data used to train and test it, so compare prediction error with the published reproducibility of the primary test method. For petroleum products, [ASTM D6122](https://store.astm.org/d6122-25.html) sets out how to validate multivariate analyzer predictions against the primary test method, and [ASTM D6708](https://store.astm.org/d6708-24.html) provides statistics for assessing agreement between two methods that measure the same property. These practices are written for infrared and Raman analyzers, not NMR, but their logic carries over: representative samples, an independent validation set, and acceptance criteria agreed before testing starts.

  NIR estimates crude properties by relating a sample's spectrum to calibration data, and it has a credible track record: [IFP researchers](https://doi.org/10.2516/ogst:1999040) demonstrated multivariate NIR predictions of middle-distillate properties, and validation of IR analyzers is standardized. NIR produces reliable results for crude types represented in its training set, but accuracy often declines when composition falls outside that range. New crude families may require additional reference samples and recalibration.

  High-Field NMR provides greater raw spectral resolution than low-field instruments, which helps specialists separate overlapping chemical signals. Raw resolution does not automatically produce more accurate operational predictions. Analysts still need to interpret the spectrum and connect its features to the properties a refinery uses.

  [Rombo AI](https://rombo.ai/product/) combines benchtop NMR with pre-trained models that interpret spectral patterns and estimate crude properties. Rombo does not quote a single accuracy figure across properties: performance is established per property in a pilot, against your reference methods, with the metrics agreed before the pilot starts. Unseen crude types, instrument conditions, and reference data quality all affect results, so the validation set should include the crudes you actually process.

  ## Operational complexity: instruments, staffing, and multi-site rollout

  Laboratory testing requires the most coordination across methods and people. Specialist technicians prepare samples, run separate assays, review results, and maintain quality controls. A multi-site program also needs shared procedures, reference materials, and proficiency testing so each laboratory produces comparable results.

  NIR requires less operator expertise during routine screening, but its calibration models create ongoing work. Each model depends on representative crude samples paired with laboratory reference values. New crude types, instrument changes, and environmental differences can require model updates or calibration transfer before another site can trust the results.

  High-field NMR requires trained spectroscopists and specialized infrastructure. Operators must maintain the instrument, control sample preparation, and interpret detailed spectra. Replicating that capability across facilities can require major equipment purchases, site preparation, method validation, and local expertise.

  ## Verdict: which method fits which team

  Choose laboratory testing when regulatory certification or contract settlement requires standardized, traceable assay results. External labs remain appropriate for one-off certification despite their higher cost and longer turnaround.

  Choose NIR when operators need rapid screening within a stable range of known crude types, when a validated calibration already exists, or when the measurement must run online in the process. NIR works well for routine checks after calibration, but new crude types and instrument drift can require model maintenance.

  Choose high-field NMR when refinery R&D needs detailed structural analysis or maximum spectral resolution. The method suits specialized investigations that justify expensive equipment, skilled operators, and slower interpretation.

  Choose Rombo AI when refineries need frequent monitoring of several properties across sites or mixed benchtop NMR fleets, and are prepared to validate performance on their own crude range first. Keep certified laboratory methods where regulations or contracts require them.
